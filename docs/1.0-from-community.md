# 1.0 — features worth building

Ordered by: does anyone else do it, and does it fit "we change your display, never the game".

---

## 1. Control the monitor itself over DDC/CI

The biggest gap in the product line, and nothing in this space does it.

Windows has a Monitor Configuration API (`dxva2.dll` — `SetMonitorBrightness`,
`SetMonitorContrast`, `SetMonitorRedGreenOrBlueGain`, and raw `SetVCPFeature` for
everything else). It talks to the monitor's own firmware over the display cable — the same
settings you'd reach through the OSD buttons.

That means PlexusX can change **the monitor's real brightness, contrast, colour gain and
built-in picture preset**, per game, automatically. Not a GPU filter sitting in front of the
image: the panel's own settings.

Why it matters here:

- It's further from the game than anything we already do. The GPU isn't even involved.
- It solves what people actually want when they ask for "brighter dark corners" — turn up
  the monitor's real brightness, the way they'd do it by hand, instead of touching the
  game's gamma. Same result they wanted, none of the problem.
- Nobody wants to press OSD buttons. Every player who has ever raised brightness for a dark
  game and forgotten to put it back is a customer for this.

Caveats to handle: many monitors ship with DDC/CI **off** in the OSD, and some laptop panels
don't expose it at all. So it needs proper detection and a clear "your monitor doesn't
support this" state, not a silent failure.

**Ship as:** per-game monitor profile — brightness, contrast, preset. Applied on launch,
restored on exit, same as vibrance.

## 2. Stretched and custom resolution, per game

Asked for three times independently in the log, once specifically for Valorant.

Setting it by hand is where people break it — they get black bars, which is every downside
of a stretched resolution and none of the upside, and then blame the resolution.

**Ship as:**
- Create the custom resolution through the driver
- Set GPU scaling to full-panel and override the game's own scaling
- Applied per game on launch, reverted on exit
- Offer the points on the scale nobody names. Everyone knows 4:3 and 16:9. **1600×1080 keeps
  112° of horizontal view against 106° at 4:3, and still stretches models 1.20×.** That
  middle ground is the actual answer for most people and no guide mentions it.
- Say plainly that a spray learned at one aspect ratio doesn't transfer to another

## 3. A benchmark that measures instead of scoring

Every optimizer shows a made-up number going up. None of them measure anything.

Build the opposite: capture frametimes for a real minute of play, apply a tweak, capture
again, and show **average and 1% lows, before and after, on this machine**.

Two reasons this is the best idea in the list:

- It's the only honest answer to "does this actually do anything", and it's a claim no
  competitor can copy without exposing that their toggles do nothing
- It protects us. A tweak that turns out to cost frames on some hardware gets caught by
  users instead of becoming a refund. We already had a toggle that was doing the opposite of
  what it claimed — this is what would have found it.

**Ship as:** a Measure button next to the tweaks. It should be allowed to report that a
tweak did nothing.

## 4. Profile share codes

Serialise a profile — vibrance, saturation, resolution, monitor settings, crosshair — into a
short string. Paste someone's code, get their setup.

Cheap to build, and it spreads on its own. Every "what are your settings" message in the
server becomes a code instead of a screenshot, and every code has the app's name attached
to it.

---

## 5. Per-game sharpening

NVIDIA and AMD both expose image sharpening at driver level. It pairs with vibrance for the
same reason people want vibrance — separating a player from the texture behind them — and
it's another slider that lives outside the game.

Worth confirming what's reachable per-GPU before committing to it.

---

## 6. Colour vision modes

Roughly one in twelve men has some form of colour blindness. In a 300-person server that's
around twenty people, and for them the whole product is currently tuned wrong — cranking
saturation on channels they can't separate does nothing.

Proper daltonisation for deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia is a display transform, the
same class of thing we already ship. Nobody in this market does it. It's a genuine reason to
be the only option for those people rather than one of several.

## Not building: "bright night"

Requested once and argued down in the channel by another member before we replied. He was
right.

Making dark corners visible is what got the colour plugins people already mentioned banned.
Doing it at the monitor rather than in-game changes the mechanism, not how it reads to a
moderator.

Note the difference from item 1: raising the monitor's actual brightness is a thing every
player can already do with the buttons on their screen, and does. Remapping black to green
is not. The first is convenience, the second is an advantage nobody else has — that's the
line, and it's worth being able to say out loud.

## Loose ends from the log

- **Prices don't match.** Channel says 3 EUR/month and 8–12 lifetime. Site says $4 and $12.
  People screenshot this.
- **Site overclaims on GPUs.** The pinned info post is precise — Saturation works on any
  GPU, Vibrance is NVIDIA-only. The site implies everything works everywhere. An AMD buyer
  who finds that out after paying is a refund and a bad post.
- **Nine real testimonials now exist** in the feedback channel. Ask each person, then they
  replace the placeholders on the site.
- **One bug report:** Rust loading slowly at startup, never diagnosed.
- **The real competitor is a belief**, not a product: "many people believe nvidia is the
  same". Every piece of marketing should be built to beat that sentence.
